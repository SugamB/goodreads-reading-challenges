Goodreads Challenge Optimizer Help & FAQ

What is the Goodreads Challenge Optimizer?

The Goodreads Challenge Optimizer is a lightweight planning tool designed to help readers organize their To-Be-Read (TBR) lists for seasonal and custom reading challenges. By mapping out books that fulfill multiple categories, it helps you finish your reading goals with fewer books.

How does the "Minimize Total Pages" optimization work?

When activated, our engine solves a Weighted Set Cover problem by running 80 fast iterations behind the scenes. It targets books with the lowest page-to-challenge ratio to construct the absolute shortest reading path mathematically possible, without requiring you to keep refreshing.

Can I plan a challenge list with zero book overlaps?

Yes. If you prefer to read a distinct, unique book for every single category, click "One per challenge". The optimization engine recalculates your plan to ensure exactly one unique book is recommended per challenge prompt. If you pin an overlapping book, the planner schedules it last in your reading order so it collects only the one remaining bookmark — keeping every read at exactly one new achievement.

What is Lock Impact ("Wiggle Room") analysis?

If you lock in "Must Read" books, the optimizer checks how many pages those commitments add compared to the absolute shortest path. It calculates the exact reading penalty of each locked book and lets you unlock them with one click if you want to save time.

How does the Goodreads CSV Import work?

Clicking Import Goodreads CSV opens a short guide with a direct link to Goodreads' export page. Once you upload your exported library file, our parser processes your shelves locally in your browser so you can cross-reference your own TBR shelves or owned books against the challenge list.

About this tool, data sources & privacy

The Goodreads Challenge Optimizer is a free, browser-based dashboard that compares multiple Goodreads reading-challenge lists, finds books that count toward several challenges at once, and ranks them so readers can finish more challenges with fewer books. Each book record carries Goodreads' own data: page count, average rating, ratings count, publication year and month, format, and community genre tags. All filtering and library import run locally in the browser — nothing is uploaded.

As of September 2026, the public dataset is refreshed whenever the curator re-scans the challenge pages, and the companion "Goodreads Book Details" extension (Chrome/Firefox, Manifest V3) adds the same details inline on Goodreads pages themselves.

Does it work on phones?
Yes — the web app runs in any phone or tablet browser with no install. The browser extension is desktop-only (Chrome and Firefox).

Can I filter by genre, e.g. Young Adult only?
Yes — the Genres filter in the filter bar (bottom sheet on phones) includes or excludes Goodreads community tags such as Young Adult, and it applies to Browse, the Optimal path and (optionally) the spreadsheet. The Overlap Spreadsheet itself stays a full catalog of your selected challenges by default — same rows as the original Google Sheet — with an optional "Respect filters" toggle in its header. Goodreads genres are community-applied tags and shelves, not official age ratings, so skim before handing a pick to a younger reader.

Do I need a Goodreads account? Is my data uploaded?
No account is needed for the public dataset. Your library CSV never leaves the browser — parsing and filtering are fully local.

Where does the book data come from?
From public Goodreads challenge and list pages, captured by the curator's scanner and enriched from each book's public Goodreads page (including its __NEXT_DATA__ genre payload and editions list for page counts).

Export your Goodreads Library

How to get your CSV file:
  1. On desktop, go to My Books → Import and export (under Tools), or use the button below.
  2. Under Export, click Export Library — large libraries can take a few minutes to generate.
  3. When ready, click the "Your export from [date]" link to download your .csv file.
